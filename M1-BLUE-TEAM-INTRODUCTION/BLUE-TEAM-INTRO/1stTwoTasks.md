# SOC Role in Blue Team — Overall Notes

## 1. Main Concept

The SOC is part of the Blue Team, which focuses on defensive security. Different security teams and roles work together to monitor, detect, investigate, and respond to cyber threats. Starting as a SOC L1 can provide a strong foundation for progressing into L2 or other specialized cybersecurity roles.

## 2. Important Things to Remember

- **CEO:** Focuses on overall business objectives.
- **CISO:** Oversees the organization's cybersecurity strategy and security teams.
- Security departments can include:
  - **Red Team:** Finds security weaknesses through offensive security.
  - **GRC:** Handles governance, risk, policies, and compliance.
  - **Blue Team:** Defends the organization against threats.

### Blue Team / SOC

- **SOC:** Central defensive security team and first line of defense.
- **L1 Analyst:** Monitors, triages, and performs initial investigation of alerts. Escalates complex cases to L2 when needed.
- **L2 Analyst:** Investigates more advanced and complex attacks.
- **SOC Engineer:** Configures and maintains tools such as SIEM and EDR and helps improve detection.
- **SOC Manager:** Manages and coordinates the SOC team.
- SOC may cooperate with IT teams and help create/improve detection rules.

### Serious Incidents

- **CIRT / CSIRT / CERT:** Handles serious or critical incidents when the incident goes beyond normal SOC capabilities.
- May involve specialists such as digital forensics analysts, threat intelligence analysts, threat hunters, and malware analysts.

### SOC Career

- SOC L1 helps build broad cybersecurity knowledge and real-world experience.
- The natural next step is often SOC L2, but you can move into other areas such as:
  - Security Engineering
  - Incident Response / CIRT
  - Digital Forensics
  - Threat Intelligence
  - AppSec
  - Security Management
- **Internal SOC:** Protects one organization; usually focuses deeply on fewer tools and environments.
- **MSSP:** Provides security services to multiple clients; usually faster-paced, with more diverse tools and incidents.
- Build skills through practice, labs/CTFs, IT knowledge, cyber news, and relevant certifications.
- The first 1–2 years are important for gaining real-world experience and discovering your preferred career path.

## What I Should Understand After This Module

I should understand where a SOC L1 fits within the larger cybersecurity and Blue Team structure, what happens when an alert becomes more complex or serious, and the basic path:

**L1 Alert Triage → L2 Complex Investigation → CIRT for major/critical incidents**

I should also understand that SOC L1 is not a dead-end role—it can be a starting point for multiple cybersecurity career paths.
